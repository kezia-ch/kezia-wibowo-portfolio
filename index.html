Nata Supermarket customer analytics

Who shops at Nata, which campaigns they respond to, and how much stock each product line needs next period.

What we delivered

Python notebooks for cleaning, clustering and response modelling, an Excel forecast, a Power BI dashboard and a final presentation.

Open project files

How we did it

  1. 1 Clean the customer dataFilled missing income values with averages, removed an implausible income entry of 666,666, standardised odd marital status values, and encoded categorical fields.
  2. 2 Segment customersRan K-means clustering and chose three clusters by weighing the elbow method, silhouette scores, cluster balance and how easy each segment is to act on.
  3. 3 Model campaign responseBuilt decision tree models for each of five past campaigns to find what drives acceptance, and compared decision trees with KNN inside each segment.
  4. 4 Forecast and recommendForecast next-period demand per product category with exponential smoothing in Excel and Power BI, then wrote a channel and offer plan for each segment.

Digital promo shoppers, about 26%Upper-middle income, the most web purchases and the most deal-driven.
High-value boutique customers, about 24%Highest income and spending, store and catalogue led, most responsive to campaigns.
Price-sensitive infrequents, about 50%Lowest income and spending, the most website visits but few purchases.

Team projectStatistical Modeling for BusinessR

What makes airline passengers satisfied?

A statistical study of nearly 130,000 passenger surveys to find which services actually drive satisfaction, and where the airline should invest first.

What we delivered

An R Markdown analysis and a 29-page report with findings, business implications and recommendations.

Open project files

How we did it

  1. 1 Clean and transformMerged the train and test files into 129,880 passenger records, dropped the 0.3% of rows missing arrival delay, log-transformed the skewed delay variables and trimmed outliers with the IQR method.
  2. 2 Explore and testChecked normality with Q-Q plots, then used t-tests, chi-square tests and confidence intervals. Travel class split satisfaction most sharply: Economy passengers were satisfied only 18.9% of the time.
  3. 3 Model the driversFitted simple and multiple logistic regressions (McFadden's pseudo R² of 0.331) and confirmed with VIF scores below 3 that the predictors weren't collinear.
  4. 4 Turn it into strategyUsed PCA to condense 14 service ratings into three pillars explaining 59.5% of the variance, and closed with four recommendations, starting with Economy's online boarding.

What moves satisfaction most

Online boarding×2.31
Leg room×1.46
Inflight entertainment×1.36

Odds ratios from the multiple logistic regression: how much one extra rating point multiplies the odds of a satisfied passenger

Credit card approval prediction

An end-to-end machine learning project that predicts whether a credit card application will be approved, using applicant demographic and financial records.

View code on GitHubOpen project files

How we did it

  1. 1 Clean and mergeMerged the application and credit records on client ID into one accepted or rejected status, recategorised 134,000+ missing occupation values, and turned backward-counted days into age and years employed.
  2. 2 Check what's missingCompared acceptance rates for missing and known occupations (8.8% versus 8.2%) to confirm the gaps were missing completely at random before removing them.
  3. 3 Explore and correlateBinned years employed, compared accepted and rejected profiles with bar and pie charts, and ranked every feature's correlation with approval.
  4. 4 ModelSet a logistic regression baseline, moved to a random forest for non-linear patterns, and extracted the top 20 features driving approval.
